*
* `nvme list` output is ordered by discovery, and the client's root EBS volume is
* an NVMe device too -- it appears as /dev/nvme0n1 in AWS's own example, with the
* ONTAP namespace at nvme2n1. **Writing to the wrong node destroys the client.**
*
* So `runbook.sh block-provision nvme` resolves the namespace with
* `nvme netapp ontapdevices`, which maps a namespace path to its device, and
* rewrites this line. `runbook.sh block-preflight` refuses to proceed until the
* path here is the ONTAP namespace.

**********************************************************************
* Multipathing is not the same mechanism as on the iSCSI side, and it is part of
* the measurement rather than a detail of the setup.
*
* iSCSI uses dm-multipath. NVMe/TCP uses ANA, and the two paths are **not
* symmetric**: the node holding the namespace reports `optimized` and the other
* reports `non-optimized`, with an iopolicy of round-robin across them. So the
* load is not split the way dm-multipath splits it.
*
* **That asymmetry is a candidate explanation for any difference F-2 finds**, which
* is why the preflight records `iopolicy` and both paths' ANA states, and why the
* result carries them. Without them a protocol difference and a path-distribution
* difference are indistinguishable.
*
* No size=, and the fill pass applies here too -- an unwritten namespace returns
* zeros without reaching disk. Same reasoning as the iSCSI file.
**********************************************************************
